## Question 

What is a computer network? Explain its major goals and real-world uses.

## Answer 

A computer network is a collection of interconnected computing devices (such as computers, smartphones, servers, and printers) that communicate and share data, applications, and hardware resources over wired or wireless connections. 

### Major Goals of a Computer Network

* Resource Sharing: Allows all connected users to access and use physical or digital assets—such as printers, scanners, databases, and cloud storage—without duplicating them. 
* Cost Reduction: Lowers expenses by enabling multiple users to share a single hardware device (like a printer) or software license stored on a centralized server rather than buying individual setups for every machine.
* High Reliability: Ensures data availability and fault tolerance through backup systems and file replication, meaning that if one device or node fails, operations can continue via redundant paths. 
* Fast Communication: Provides rapid and reliable communication channels—including email, instant messaging, voice calls, and video conferencing—to connect people regardless of physical distance. 
* Scalability and Performance: Enables organizations to expand their computing power or add new nodes, servers, and workstations dynamically as demands grow. 

### Real-World Uses

* Business Collaboration & Remote Work: Companies use local area networks (LANs) and wide area networks (WANs) to let employees share files, access enterprise resource planning (ERP) software, and host video meetings from any location.
* Banking and Financial Services: Financial institutions rely on global networks for electronic fund transfers (EFT), automated teller machines (ATMs), credit card verifications, and real-time stock trading.
* Healthcare & Patient Management: Hospitals use secure networks to store and retrieve electronic health records (EHR), manage diagnostic imaging, and coordinate care across departments.
* Education & Distance Learning: Schools and universities utilize campus networks and internet connectivity to deliver online classes, virtual libraries, and collaborative research platforms.
* Manufacturing & Design: Industrial networks power computer-aided design (CAD) and computer-aided manufacturing (CAM), allowing multiple engineers to work on product blueprints and machine automation simultaneously.
* Home & Personal Entertainment: Everyday households use Wi-Fi networks to connect smart TVs, phones, and laptops to stream movies, play online multiplayer games, and control smart-home devices.
