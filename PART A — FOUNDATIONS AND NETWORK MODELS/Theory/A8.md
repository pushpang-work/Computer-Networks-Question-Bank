## Question 

Compare guided and unguided transmission media.

## Solution 

Guided and unguided transmission media are the two main channels used to send data from a sender to a receiver in computer networks. 

### Quick Comparison

| Feature | Guided Media (Wired) | Unguided Media (Wireless) |
|---|---|---|
| Path Type | Uses physical wires or cables | Uses air, water, or vacuum (no physical wire) |
| Also Known As | Bounded or wired media | Unbounded or wireless media |
| Signal Form | Electrical voltages, current, or light (photons) | Electromagnetic waves (radio, microwave, infrared) |
| Direction / Scope | Point-to-point (confined to the physical path) | Omnidirectional or line-of-sight broadcasting |
| Security | High (harder to tap into physical cables) | Lower (signals travel through open air, easier to intercept) |
| Speed & Bandwidth | Generally higher speeds and cleaner bandwidth | Generally lower speeds, prone to environmental interference |
| Common Examples | Twisted-pair, coaxial cable, fiber optics | Radio waves, microwaves, infrared |

### Guided Transmission Media
Guided media confines and directs data signals along a solid, physical path. 

* How it works: Signals travel through a physical medium like copper or glass.  
* Types:
* Twisted-Pair Cable: Two copper wires twisted together to reduce crosstalk; widely used in LANs and telephone lines.
   * Coaxial Cable: A central copper conductor surrounded by insulation and a metal shield; used for cable television and broadband internet.
   * Fiber-Optic Cable: Thin glass or plastic strands that transmit data as pulses of light; offers the highest speeds and longest distance capacity (the internet backbone). 

### Unguided Transmission Media
Unguided media transmits data signals through the air (or space) without any physical conductor. 

* How it works: Signals broadcast outward as unconfined electromagnetic waves.  
* Types:
* Radio Waves: Omnidirectional waves that travel easily over long distances; used for FM radio, mobile phones, and Wi-Fi.
   * Microwaves: High-frequency, directional (line-of-sight) waves used for cellular networks, radar, and satellite communications.
   * Infrared: Short-range, high-frequency waves used for TV remotes, wireless mice, and short-distance data sharing.
