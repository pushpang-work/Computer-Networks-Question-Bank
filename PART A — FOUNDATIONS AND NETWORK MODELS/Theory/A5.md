## Question 

Define bandwidth, throughput, goodput, latency, jitter, and packet loss. Explain how they affect a video call.

## Answer 

### Network Metrics Defined

* Bandwidth: The maximum theoretical amount of data that a network link can carry at one time, measured in bits per second (e.g., Mbps or Gbps). Think of it as the width of a pipe. 
* Throughput: The actual amount of data successfully transferred across the network at any given moment, which is usually lower than the bandwidth due to congestion or limits. Think of it as the actual flow of traffic through the pipe. 
* Goodput: The measure of useful application-level data delivered per second, excluding overhead like retransmitted packets, duplicate data, or protocol headers. 
* Latency: The time delay it takes for a single packet of data to travel from its source to its destination, typically measured in milliseconds (ms).  
* Jitter: The statistical variation or inconsistency in packet delay over time. If packets arrive at irregular intervals instead of a steady flow, the network has high jitter. 
* Packet Loss: The percentage of data packets that fail to reach their destination altogether. 

### How They Affect a Video Call
Video calls are real-time, bidirectional applications that demand a steady flow of synchronized audio and video. These six metrics interact to shape the live experience: 

* Bandwidth & Throughput:
    * Effect: If your throughput falls below what the video codec requires (e.g., roughly 1.5–3 Mbps for standard HD video), the call will automatically drop to a lower resolution, blur, or stall. You need enough throughput in both directions (upload and download) to send your video/audio and receive the other person's.
* Goodput:
    * Effect: Even if raw throughput looks decent on a speed test, high network errors mean the system wastes capacity resending broken data. Low goodput results in frozen frames or sudden drops in call clarity because actual usable data isn't keeping pace.
* Latency:
    * Effect: High latency (e.g., >150 ms) creates an awkward satellite-phone-style delay where participants talk over each other. While a 200 ms delay is manageable, it ruins the feeling of a natural, instantaneous conversation. 
* Jitter:
    * Effect: Video apps use a "jitter buffer" to smooth out minor arrival time differences. However, if jitter exceeds ~30–50 ms, the buffer overflows or underflows. This causes robotic or clipped audio, stuttering video movements, and out-of-sync lip movements. [
* Packet Loss:
    * Effect: Even a small amount of packet loss (above 1%) can badly degrade a call. Because real-time video cannot wait long for lost data to be retransmitted without freezing, missing packets result in grey video blocks, momentary freezes, or chopped-off words.
