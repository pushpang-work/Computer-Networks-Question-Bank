## Question 

Compare PAN, LAN, CAN, MAN, WAN, and the Internet with examples.

## Answer 

Computer networks are classified primarily by their geographic scale, ownership, and the range of devices they connect. 

### Comparison of Network Types

| Network Type | Full Form | Coverage Range | Primary Media | Common Ownership |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PAN | Personal Area Network | Less than 10 meters (33 feet) | Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, USB, NFC | Individual user |
| LAN | Local Area Network | Single room, building, or home | Ethernet, Wi-Fi | Private (home/company) |
| CAN | Campus Area Network | Few kilometers (interconnected buildings) | Fiber optic backbones | Organization, university, or enterprise |
| MAN | Metropolitan Area Network | 5 to 50 kilometers (city-wide) | Fiber optics, microwave | ISP or local government |
| WAN | Wide Area Network | Above 50 km to global (countries/continents) | Leased lines, satellite, fiber | Multiple providers / enterprise |
| The Internet | Global Network System | Worldwide (Interconnected WANs) | Undersea cables, satellites, fiber | Decentralized / public-private infrastructure |


### Detailed Breakdown & Examples

### 1. PAN (Personal Area Network)

* Definition: The smallest network type, centered around a single person and their immediate personal workspace.
* Key Features: Low power, short range (under 10 meters), usually temporary.
* Example: Connecting your smartphone to a wireless smartwatch and Bluetooth earbuds, or tethering your laptop via mobile hotspot.

### 2. LAN (Local Area Network)

* Definition: Connects computers and digital devices within a limited, confined physical area like a house, office, or school.
* Key Features: High data-transfer speeds, low cost, easy to set up, uses local routers and switches.
* Example: Your home Wi-Fi network connecting your smart TV, printer, and laptops, or an office floor sharing a local network server. 

### 3. CAN (Campus Area Network)

* Definition: Larger than a LAN, a CAN interconnects multiple local area networks across a specific, contiguous cluster of buildings.
* Key Features: Faster than a MAN or WAN because the organization owns the internal cabling (often high-speed fiber backbones).
* Example: A university campus connecting the engineering building, library, and dormitories into one unified network, or a large corporate tech park.  

### 4. MAN (Metropolitan Area Network)

* Definition: Spans an entire town or city (5 to 50 km), bridging multiple LANs and CANs together.
* Key Features: Higher complexity and cost than a LAN; often managed by telecommunication companies or municipal bodies.
* Example: A city-wide cable television network or a municipal free Wi-Fi infrastructure deployed across downtown streets. 

### 5. WAN (Wide Area Network)

* Definition: Spans vast geographic distances, connecting multiple smaller networks (LANs and MANs) across cities, states, or countries.
* Key Features: Relies on third-party carriers, leased telecommunication lines, satellites, or MPLS links; higher latency and setup cost.
* Example: A multinational corporation connecting its New York headquarters with branch offices in Tokyo and London.

### 6. The Internet

* Definition: The ultimate, massive "network of networks"—a global system of interconnected WANs that links billions of devices worldwide.
* Key Features: Decentralized, publicly accessible, using standardized communication protocols (TCP/IP).
* Example: Accessing a website hosted in a different country, sending an email globally, or streaming online video.
