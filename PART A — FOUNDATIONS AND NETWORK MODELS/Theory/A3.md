## Question 

Explain bus, star, ring, mesh, tree, and hybrid topologies. Give one advantage and limitation of each.

## Answer 

Network topologies describe how devices and cables connect to form a computer network. 

### Bus Topology
All devices connect to a single central cable called the backbone. 

* Advantage: Simple and inexpensive to set up with low cable usage.
* Limitation: If the main backbone cable fails, the entire network goes down. 

### Star Topology
All devices connect individually to a central hub, switch, or router. 

* Advantage: Easy to add or remove devices without disrupting the rest of the network.
* Limitation: If the central hub or switch fails, the entire network halts.

### Ring Topology
Devices connect in a closed circular loop where data travels from one device to the next. 

* Advantage: Orderly transmission with reduced data collisions since data moves in a single direction.
* Limitation: A single device or cable failure can disrupt or break the entire ring. 

### Mesh Topology
Devices are interconnected with multiple direct paths between them (either fully or partially connected).

* Advantage: High reliability and fault tolerance because data can reroute through alternative paths if a link fails.
* Limitation: Very expensive and complex to install due to high cabling and port requirements. 

### Tree Topology
A hierarchical structure that combines star and bus topologies into parent-child branches. 

* Advantage: Easily scalable, allowing new star-structured branches to be added without affecting existing nodes.
* Limitation: More complex to configure and manage, and if the main root node fails, upper-level branches go down. 

### Hybrid Topology
A combination of two or more distinct basic topologies (such as a mix of star and ring or star and bus).

* Advantage: Extremely flexible and scalable, letting administrators maximize strengths and neutralize weaknesses of each layout.
* Limitation: High design and administration complexity along with expensive infrastructure and hardware costs.
