# PART A — FOUNDATIONS AND NETWORK MODELS

### Theory questions

1. What is a computer network? Explain its major goals and real-world uses.
2. Compare PAN, LAN, CAN, MAN, WAN, and the Internet with examples.
3. Explain bus, star, ring, mesh, tree, and hybrid topologies. Give one advantage and limitation of each.
4. Compare client-server and peer-to-peer architectures.
5. Define bandwidth, throughput, goodput, latency, jitter, and packet loss. Explain how they affect a video call.
6. Differentiate circuit switching, message switching, and packet switching.
7. Explain simplex, half-duplex, and full-duplex communication.
8. Compare guided and unguided transmission media.
9. Describe the OSI model and the responsibility of each of its seven layers.
10. Describe the TCP/IP model and map its layers to the OSI model.
11. What are encapsulation and decapsulation? Trace a web request through the protocol layers.
12. Define protocol, service, interface, frame, packet, segment, datagram, and PDU.
13. Explain why standards bodies such as IEEE, IETF, ISO, and ITU matter.
14. Compare connection-oriented and connectionless communication.
15. Explain unicast, broadcast, multicast, and anycast.

### Practical tasks
16. Draw a labelled diagram of a home network and identify the modem/ONT, router, switch, access point, clients, and Internet connection.
17. Build a small star topology in a network simulator with at least four hosts and one switch.
18. Create an OSI-to-TCP/IP mapping table with example protocols at each layer.
19. Use ping to measure round-trip time to your router and a public host; record results over 10 attempts and explain variation.
20. Compare wired and Wi-Fi performance in your own environment using a consistent test method; document limitations.
