## Question 

What are propagation delay, transmission delay, processing delay, and queuing delay?

## Answer 

In computer networks, delay (or latency) is the total time it takes for a data packet to travel from a sender to a receiver. It is divided into four main components: 

### 1. Transmission Delay

* Definition: The time a network device (like a host or router) takes to push all the bits of a packet onto the physical transmission link.
* Formula: $T_t = \frac{L}{R}$ (where L is packet length/size in bits, and R is link bandwidth/rate in bits per second).
* Key Factor: Depends directly on packet size and inversely on the link's speed (bandwidth). 

### 2. Propagation Delay

* Definition: The time it takes for a signal (bit) to travel through the physical medium (such as copper wire, optical fiber, or air) from the sender to the receiver.
* Formula: $T_p = \frac{d}{v}$  (where d is the distance between sender and receiver, and v is the propagation speed of the signal in that medium).
* Key Factor: Depends on the physical distance and the medium's propagation speed. 

### 3. Processing Delay

* Definition: The time required by a network node (like a router or switch) to examine the packet's header, check for bit-level errors, update fields like Time-To-Live (TTL), and determine the next output link.
* Formula: No fixed mathematical formula; varies based on router hardware and software efficiency.
* Key Factor: Depends on the processing speed of the device and protocol complexity. 

### 4. Queuing Delay

* Definition: The time a packet spends waiting in a router's buffer (queue) before it can be transmitted.
* Formula: Varies dynamically depending on network congestion and arrival rates.
* Key Factor: Increases heavily when the rate of incoming packets exceeds the capacity of the outgoing link. 

### Total Nodal Delay
The overall end-to-end delay at a single node is the sum of these four parts: 
<pre>Total Delay = Processing Delay + Queuing Delay + Transmission Delay + Propagation Delay </pre>
