## Question 

Compare twisted-pair, coaxial, and optical-fiber cables.

## Answer 

Twisted pair, coaxial, and fiber optic cables differ primarily in their transmission medium, speed and bandwidth, maximum distance, and resistance to interference.

### Core Differences Comparison

| Feature | Twisted Pair Cable | Coaxial Cable | Fiber Optic Cable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transmission Medium | Insulated copper wires (UTP/STP) | Central copper conductor with shielding | Glass or plastic strands (light pulses) |
| Signal Type | Electrical signals | Electrical signals | Optical (light) signals |
| Maximum Bandwidth / Speed | 10 Mbps to 10 Gbps (up to 40 Gbps in Cat8) | 100 Mbps to 10 Gbps (via DOCSIS) | 1 Gbps to 100+ Tbps (100+ Gbps typical) |
| Maximum Distance | ~100 meters | ~500 meters to 10 km (depending on setup) | 100+ km (single-mode) |
| Electromagnetic Interference (EMI) | High susceptibility (poor noise resistance) | Moderate resistance (good shielding) | Complete immunity (unaffected by EMI) |
| Cost | Lowest | Moderate | Highest |
| Installation Difficulty | Very easy / flexible | Fairly easy / moderately bulky | Complex, fragile, and harder to splice |
| Common Uses | LAN office networks, Ethernet, telephone wiring | Cable TV (CATV), broadband internet drops, CCTV | Data centers, long-distance backbones, FTTH (Fiber-to-the-Home) |


### Key Breakdown by Type

### 1. Twisted Pair Cable

* How it works: Uses pairs of copper wires twisted together to cancel out electromagnetic crosstalk and external noise. Comes in UTP (Unshielded) and STP (Shielded) variants.
* Strengths: Inexpensive, flexible, and very simple to terminate and install in homes and offices.
* Weaknesses: Short effective run limit (typically 100 meters) and high attenuation over distance. 

### 2. Coaxial Cable ("Coax")

* How it works: Features a stiff central copper core surrounded by an insulating layer, a metallic braided shield, and an outer protective jacket.
* Strengths: Better shielding against noise than twisted pair and supports longer cable runs (up to several hundred meters) without heavy signal degradation.
* Weaknesses: Bulkier, less flexible, and gradually being phased out for high-speed local data in favor of fiber or high-category copper. 

### 3. Fiber Optic Cable

* How it works: Transmits data as pulses of light through ultra-thin strands of high-purity glass or plastic using total internal reflection.
* Strengths: Massive bandwidth capacity, multi-kilometer reach without repeaters, zero vulnerability to electrical noise or lightning strikes, and high security against wiretapping.
* Weaknesses: Expensive materials, fragile glass cores that require careful handling, and specialized tools/training for splicing and installation.
