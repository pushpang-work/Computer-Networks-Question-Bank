## Question 

Define frequency, amplitude, phase, wavelength, bit rate, and baud rate.

## Answer 

* Frequency: The number of complete wave cycles or oscillations that occur in one second, measured in hertz (Hz). 
* Amplitude: The maximum height, peak intensity, or displacement of a wave from its resting (zero) position, representing the energy or strength of the signal. 
* Phase: The position of a wave point in its cycle at a specific time, measured in degrees or radians, which indicates the timing offset relative to a reference wave. 
* Wavelength: The physical distance between two identical points (such as two consecutive crests or troughs) on a repeating wave, typically denoted by $\lambda$. 
* Bit Rate: The total number of individual binary digits (bits: 0s and 1s) transmitted or processed per second, measured in bits per second (bps). 
* Baud Rate: The number of distinct signal units, changes, or symbols transmitted per second across a communication channel, measured in baud.
* Relationship: Bit rate equals baud rate multiplied by the number of bits encoded in each symbol (Bit Rate = Baud Rate × Bits per Symbol).
