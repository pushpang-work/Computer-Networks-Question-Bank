## Question 

Explain FDM, TDM, WDM, and CDM.

## Answer 

FDM, TDM, WDM, and CDM are multiplexing techniques used to combine multiple independent signals so they can travel simultaneously over a single communication channel or medium. 

### 1. FDM (Frequency Division Multiplexing)

* How it works: The total available bandwidth is divided into non-overlapping frequency bands. Each sender is assigned a specific frequency channel to transmit data concurrently. Guard bands are used between channels to prevent signals from overlapping or causing cross-talk. 
* Type: Mostly used for analog systems. 
* Example: Traditional radio and television broadcasting (where each station occupies a distinct frequency). 

### 2. TDM (Time Division Multiplexing)

* How it works: The shared channel is divided into time slots. Users take turns transmitting data over the entire bandwidth for a brief, pre-allocated time slice in a repeating cycle.  
* Types:
    * Synchronous TDM: Assigns fixed time slots to each source whether they have data to send or not (can be wasteful if empty).
   * Asynchronous / Statistical TDM: Allocates time slots dynamically based on active demand.  
* Type: Works with both analog and digital signals (best suited for digital).  
* Example: Classic telephone networks and digital transmission links (like PCM/T1 lines).

### 3. WDM (Wavelength Division Multiplexing)

* How it works: Multiple optical (light) signals of varying wavelengths (colors) are combined and transmitted simultaneously through a single fiber-optic cable. At the receiving end, a prism or demultiplexer separates the light back into individual wavelengths. 
* Types:
    * CWDM (Coarse WDM): Uses fewer, wider-spaced wavelengths for shorter distances and lower capacity.
   * DWDM (Dense WDM): Packs a massive number of tight wavelengths onto a single fiber for ultra-long-distance, high-capacity backbones. 
* Example: High-speed fiber-optic internet and undersea telecommunication cables.  

### 4. CDM (Code Division Multiplexing)

* How it works: All users share the entire frequency band and transmit at the same time. Instead of splitting by frequency or time, every signal is multiplied by a unique, orthogonal digital code sequence (Spread Spectrum). The receiver uses the matching code to extract the desired signal while treating other signals as background noise.
* Type: Digital technology.
* Example: 3G cellular networks (CDMA).  

### Quick Comparison Table

| Technique | Dividing Factor | Signal Type | Common Application |
|---|---|---|---|
| FDM | Frequency bands | Analog | Radio, TV broadcasting |
| TDM | Time slots | Digital / Analog | Telephony, digital circuits |
| WDM | Light wavelengths | Optical | Fiber-optic backbones |
| CDM | Unique digital codes | Digital | 3G mobile communications |
