## Question 

Explain why fiber is often preferred for long-distance high-capacity links.

## Answer 

Fiber optic cables are preferred for long-distance, high-capacity links because they transmit data using pulses of light through glass or plastic cores, which allows for vastly superior bandwidth, minimal signal loss, and complete immunity to electromagnetic interference compared to traditional copper wire. 

### Key Advantages of Fiber Optics

* Massive Bandwidth and Capacity: Fiber-optic cables handle significantly more data than copper cables, supporting single-mode transmission speeds of 10 Gbps to over 100 Gbps per channel. Technologies like Wavelength Division Multiplexing (WDM) allow multiple light signals of different colors or wavelengths to travel down a single strand simultaneously, multiplying capacity. 
* Low Attenuation (Minimal Signal Loss): Light signals experience very low transmission loss (around 0.2 dB/km) in high-quality single-mode fiber. Signals can travel 10 to 80 kilometers (or up to 600 kilometers in specialized RF-over-fiber systems) before needing amplification or repeaters, whereas copper cables lose strength within 100 meters.  
* Immunity to Electromagnetic Interference (EMI): Because fiber transmits photons (light) rather than electrons (electricity), it is completely unaffected by external noise from electrical power lines, motors, or radio signals. It also eliminates crosstalk between adjacent cables.  
* Enhanced Security: Fiber-optic signals are extremely difficult to tap or intercept without physical intrusion, and any attempt to access the core typically disrupts or alters the light signal, making breaches easily detectable.  
* Physical Lightweight Profile: Fiber cables are much thinner, lighter, and more durable over long underground or undersea routes than bulky metal copper lines.
