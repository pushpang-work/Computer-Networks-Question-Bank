## Question 

Inspect Ethernet cable categories and connector types; document which cable is used in your lab and why.

## Answer 

Ethernet networks commonly use twisted-pair copper cables terminated with RJ45 connectors (officially structured as 8P8C modular plugs). 

### Ethernet Cable Categories

* Cat 5e: Supports speeds up to 1 Gbps over 100 meters at 100 MHz; acts as a standard baseline for basic office and home setups. 
* Cat 6: Supports speeds up to 10 Gbps over shorter distances (up to 55 meters) or 1 Gbps up to 100 meters at 250 MHz; reduces crosstalk with tighter twists. 
* Cat 6a: Supports speeds up to 10 Gbps over a full 100-meter span at 500 MHz; includes enhanced shielding to prevent electromagnetic interference (EMI).
* Cat 7: Supports speeds up to 10 Gbps at 600 MHz with heavy individual and overall shielding; designed for harsh industrial environments with high electrical noise. 
* Cat 8: Supports speeds up to 40 Gbps at 2,000 MHz over short ranges (up to 30 meters); built for high-performance server rooms and data centers.

### Connector Types

* RJ45 (8P8C): The standard 8-pin modular connector used for twisted-pair copper Ethernet cables (Cat 5e through Cat 8). 
* GG45 / ARJ45: High-frequency 12-pin connectors designed for Cat 7 compatibility while remaining backward-compatible with standard RJ45 plugs.
* Fiber Optic Connectors (LC, SC, ST): Specialized latched, push-pull, or bayonet-lock connectors used when transmitting data via light pulses through glass or plastic cores rather than copper. 

### Lab Cable Usage and Rationale

* Cable Used: Cat 6a (Unshielded/Shielded Twisted Pair) with RJ45 termination.
* Why It Is Used:
    * Bandwidth & Speed: Delivers full 10 Gbps throughput across standard 100-meter structured runs without dropping to lower rates.
   * Frequency: Operates at 500 MHz (double the capacity of standard Cat 6), accommodating heavy local network traffic and test equipment.
   * Interference Resistance: Built-in shielding or tight twist geometry prevents signal degradation from adjacent power lines and lab machinery.
