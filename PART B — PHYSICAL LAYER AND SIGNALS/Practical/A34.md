## Question

Use a simulator or diagram to compare a copper Ethernet link and a fiber uplink in a campus network.

## Answer 

In a campus network, fiber optics are the definitive choice for high-speed uplinks (backbones) between switches and buildings, while copper Ethernet (UTP) is standard for local device access. 
The primary difference lies in the transmission medium: copper uses electrons (electricity) over metallic pairs, while fiber uses photons (light) through glass cores.

## Direct Technical Comparison

| Feature | Copper Ethernet Link (Cat 6 / 6A) | Fiber Optic Uplink (Single/Multimode) |
|---|---|---|
| Transmission Type | Electrical Signals (Electrons) | Light Pulses (Photons) |
| Maximum Distance | Strictly 100 meters (328 ft) | Up to 10 km+ (Single-mode) |
| Data Capacity | Typically 1 Gbps (up to 10 Gbps at short range) | 10 Gbps, 40 Gbps, to 100 Gbps+ |
| Interference (EMI) | Prone to electromagnetic & lightning damage | 100% Immune to EMI and lightning |
| Speed Negotiation | Autonegotiates (e.g., drops from 1G to 100Mbps if needed) | Fixed Speed (Optic hardware speeds must match precisely) |
| Primary Use Case | Connecting end-user devices (PCs, APs, Printers) | Core-to-Distribution and Inter-building backbones |

## Architectural Trade-Offs

## 1. Why Copper Rules the Access Layer

* Power Delivery: Copper supports Power over Ethernet (PoE), enabling the switch to power devices like IP phones, security cameras, and Wi-Fi Access Points directly through the data cable. Fiber cannot carry electrical power. 
* Cost & Termination: Copper cables are cheap, highly durable, and easily terminated or repaired on-site by standard technicians. 

## 2. Why Fiber Wins the Uplink Backbones

* Distance Defiance: Campus buildings are routinely separated by more than 100 meters. Copper experiences intense signal attenuation (fading) beyond this limit, whereas fiber can travel miles without needing a repeater.  
* Electrical Isolation: Running copper between different structures creates ground loops and turns the cable into a lightning rod. Fiber is dielectric (made of glass/plastic), completely eliminating electrical risk between buildings.
