## Question 

Design a small office cabling plan with cable runs, uplink locations, and a rationale for cable choices.

## Answer 

This small office cabling plan is designed for a typical 2,500 sq. ft. office accommodating roughly 15–20 employees, a dedicated server closet (MDF), a conference room, and a reception area. It prioritizes gigabit performance, future-proofing, and clean cable management.

### 🏢 Network Topology & Uplink Locations
The network follows a Star Topology where all drops route back to a centralized network rack.

* Main Distribution Frame (MDF) / Server Closet: Located in a central, climate-controlled, secure room. This houses the primary uplink, ISP gateway, firewall, core PoE+ switch, and a 24-port patch panel.
* Uplink Location: The main ISP fiber entry point (Demarcation or "Demarc") terminates directly into the MDF rack.
* Workstation Clusters: Cables run from individual wall jacks, through the ceiling grid, directly back to the patch panel in the MDF.

### 📊 Cable Drop Schedule & Runs
To ensure adequate coverage, each desk receives a dual-drop configuration (one for a PC, one for a VoIP phone or auxiliary device).

### ⚙️ Cable Choices & Rationale
1. Horizontal Runs (Desks & Devices): Cat6 UTP (Unshielded Twisted Pair)

    * Rationale: Cat6 supports data transfer speeds up to 10 Gbps over distances up to 165 feet (55 meters), and 1 Gbps up to 328 feet (100 meters). This easily covers the footprint of a small office. It provides excellent bandwidth for day-to-day operations while remaining highly cost-effective and flexible to install compared to Cat6A.

2. Jacket Type: CMP (Plenum-Rated)

    * Rationale: Because cable runs will pass through the drop-ceiling spaces used for HVAC airflow (plenum spaces), building codes strictly require Plenum-rated (CMP) cabling. In the event of a fire, plenum cables use low-smoke materials that resist burning and do not release toxic fumes into the air circulation system.

3. Patch Cables: Cat6 Stranded Slim-run

    * Rationale: Stranded copper offers flexibility for tight bends inside the server rack and at the desks. Slim-run variations improve airflow inside the network cabinet, preventing hardware from overheating.

### 🛠️ Installation & Pathway Best Practices

* Pathway Support: Cables must never rest directly on top of ceiling tiles or fluorescent light fixtures (which cause electromagnetic interference). Use J-hooks anchored to the structural ceiling every 4 to 5 feet to bundle and support the runs.
* Bend Radius: Maintain a minimum bend radius of 4 times the cable diameter during installation to prevent internal copper stretching and cross-talk.
* Separation: Keep data cables at least 12 inches away from high-voltage electrical lines. If they must cross, cross them at a perpendicular 90-degree angle to eliminate electrical hum/interference.
* Labeling Scheme: Implement a rigorous labeling standard on both ends. For example, a jack labeled B03 on the office floor must map exactly to port B03 on the patch panel.
